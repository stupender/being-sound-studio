# beingsound.studio — Site Audit (July 2026)

*Written against your CV, the Vision & Path document, and a full read of the
site. Print it, mark it in pen, disagree freely. Nothing here has been changed
on the site — this is assessment, and each item waits for your yes.*

**The one-sentence finding:** the site is a beautiful, calm portfolio that
already *looks* like the studio you describe — but it is built to be admired,
not to be answered. Almost every page ends without asking the visitor to do
anything. Your own vision document names the bottleneck — *"people do not know
what I do"* (the shoemaker) — and the site's job is to fix exactly that, then
offer one clear next step per page.

## 1. What is already working — don't touch

- **The visual identity.** Two colors, two typefaces, real photography,
  generous margins. It reads as considered and calm — rare, and exactly the
  register your institutional buyers evaluate ("no inflation").
- **The transport player.** Music plays while people browse. This is your
  single best proof-of-work feature: the product *is* the experience.
- **Services as the default landing page.** Most artist sites land on the
  portfolio; yours lands on the offers. That is the right conversion instinct,
  already in place.
- **Bespoke Sound page.** Genuinely strong: problem → method → use cases →
  process → deliverables → pricing anchors → testimonial → one CTA (discovery
  call). This page already follows the "service deck" logic from your vision
  doc. It is the template the rest of the site should learn from.
- **The new capture infrastructure.** /listen, the welcome page, and the
  form wiring are the missing funnel plumbing, now real.

## 2. The bio (your partner is right — and the fix is structural)

**Diagnosis.** The current Stu bio is a *credits list wearing a paragraph's
clothing*: collective → artists performed with → venues → radio placements.
Name-dropping isn't the crime; the crime is that the names are doing the work
the *story* should do. A reader finishes knowing who you've stood next to, but
not what you *do*, for whom, or why it matters. Meanwhile your actual
differentiator — the method (deep listening, sounding with), the formation
(jazz → Zen → raga → Bön), the question (*what is sound for?*) — is compressed
into half a sentence.

**The structural fix: invert the pyramid.**

1. **What I do and who it's for** (the shoemaker's sign) — 2–3 sentences.
2. **The method and the formation as a story** — the turn from performing to
   listening; the lineages named as *teachers you study with*, not chips.
   Your CV language is already perfect for this: "translator of lineages."
3. **Credits compressed into one "selected" line each** — performance,
   recording, press. The names still appear (they matter for trust), but as
   evidence at the end, not the opening argument. The full record lives on
   the Music/Collaborations pages and the CV — link to them.

**A draft to react to** (edit ruthlessly; your voice, not mine):

> Stu Pender is a guitarist, composer, and sonic artist in Chicago. His work
> begins and ends in listening — to people, to rooms, to what sound is doing
> beneath the surface of a life. Through Being Sound he composes bespoke
> soundscapes and sonic environments for practitioners, creators, and spaces,
> and teaches music as a practice of attention: guitar, voice, and embodied
> listening.
>
> He came up through jazz and a decade of performing in New York, and the
> question slowly changed shape — from *how do I succeed as a guitarist* to
> *what is sound actually for*. Answering it has meant years of Zen training,
> certification in sound and the healing arts at the New York Open Center,
> ongoing study of North Indian raga and Yoga of the Voice with Silvia
> Nakkach, and a three-year training in the Bön Five Elements tradition with
> Tenzin Wangyal Rinpoche. He works as a translator of these lineages —
> carrying what was given into forms people can actually live with.
>
> Selected performance: MoMA PS1, the Greek Theatre, Central Park
> SummerStage, the Beacon Theatre, the United Nations. Selected recording:
> Carlos Niño & Friends with Laraaji (International Anthem), Topaz Jones,
> Photay. Solo records on Youngbloods, heard on KEXP, KCRW, and Apple
> Music's Headspace playlist.

Note what happened to the names: same evidence, one-third the space, zero
name-dropping *feel* — because the story now frames them instead of the
reverse.

**Also:** the "About Being Sound" paragraph is good but ends without a door.
One line after it — "Commission a soundscape, take a lesson, or start with a
free one: /listen" — turns the About page from a cul-de-sac into a junction.

## 3. Navigation & information architecture

- **"WORK" vs "Services" is a hidden confusion.** To you, WORK = portfolio
  and Services = offers. A first-time visitor reads "Services … WORK" as two
  words for the same thing. Small rename options: keep Services; retitle the
  filter group "LISTEN" or "PORTFOLIO" — or drop the label and let
  Music/Collaborations/Apps stand alone.
- **"Calendar" (nav) vs "UPCOMING EVENTS" (page)** — same page, two names.
  Pick one ("Events" is honest and warmer than Calendar).
- **/listen and /loop have no doors.** Deliberate so far (QR-first), but once
  live, the freebie deserves one quiet link — the Services page ("A GIFT"
  group beside COMMISSION/DEVELOP/SUBSCRIBE) and the About page line above.
  Every page that mentions your music should eventually route somewhere that
  captures.
- **Three Substack links vs zero owned-list links.** Offers, teaching page,
  and a project card all point to Substack; nothing points to your own list
  yet. Substack does let you export subscribers, so it's not wasted — but the
  vision doc is explicit that *email is the spine and the only owned channel*.
  After checkpoint 5, the footer capture becomes the default door on every
  page, and Substack becomes one offer among several rather than the only ask.

## 4. The Events page

- **The empty state undermines you.** With no upcoming shows, the page leads
  with "No Upcoming Events" — a shrug where proof should be. Fix: lead with
  PAST EVENTS retitled **"Selected performances"** (they're tours — Gipsy
  Kings, Hayley Kiyoko, NIKI — that's proof, not history), and show upcoming
  only when there *is* something upcoming.
- **It's all sideman work.** Every listed event is "guitarist with X." True
  and impressive — but it argues for a career you're moving *from*. One
  Being Sound event (a Listening Hour, the Sivananda workshop from the CV, an
  Aura set) would rebalance the page toward the studio's own life.
- **This page is the natural home of /loop.** "Met me at a show? Stay in the
  loop" + the form. People who look at an events page are exactly the people
  who go to shows.

## 5. Conversion — where the leaks are

Walk each page as a visitor and ask: *what does this page want me to do?*

| Page | Current next step | Leak |
|---|---|---|
| Services | 3 doors (bespoke / lessons / Substack) | Works. Add the freebie as a 4th, zero-risk door |
| Bespoke | Book discovery call | Solid. Could add "or start smaller: commission examples" |
| Music Lessons | Book lesson / clarity call | Solid |
| Music | → Bandcamp (external) | **Visitor leaves forever.** No capture, no return path |
| Collaborations | → YouTube etc. (external) | Same leak |
| Apps | → external, or **dead links** | Same leak, plus three cards go nowhere (below) |
| Events | none | No CTA at all |
| About | contact links | Ends without a door |
| /listen · /loop · footer | **capture** | The fix, arriving |

The pattern: your *offer* pages convert fine; your *portfolio* pages —
the ones most likely to be loved and shared — send people to platforms you
don't own and keep nothing. The footer capture (checkpoint 5) plugs every one
of these at once. That's why it matters more than any single page.

**Dead links to fix:** three Apps cards (Audio Archive, Being Sound, Sound
Station) link to `#` — a click jumps to the top of the page and teaches the
visitor to stop clicking. Until each has a case study, link them to the best
thing that exists (a Substack post, a screenshot, even "in development —
ask me"), or make them visibly non-clickable.

## 6. Portfolio depth

- **Music/Collaborations: right depth.** Cards, players, press — enough.
- **Apps: one line each is too thin for the job you want them to do.** Your
  vision doc calls Archive "an institutional product." An institutional buyer
  needs 3–4 sentences: what it is, who it's for, what state it's in. The
  commented-out intro paragraph in the HTML is good — restore a version of it.
- **Bespoke: the missing artifact is one case study.** The page argues
  beautifully in the abstract. One concrete story — *the brief, what I made
  (with the audio embedded), what changed for the client* — would do more than
  any copy edit. Maia's soundscape or the Rinpoche Water commission are
  obvious candidates. This also becomes the "service deck" your vision doc
  says should gate discovery calls.
- **Testimonials exist (5) but live only inside the offer pages.** The
  strongest one deserves to appear near the *point of decision* — beside the
  discovery-call button, not paragraphs above it.

## 7. Technical & hygiene (quick wins, none urgent)

1. **Malformed root tags:** the file opens `<HTML><html lang="en">` — two
   nested html tags. Browsers forgive it; fix when convenient.
2. **No meta description** — the one line Google shows under your name.
   Worth writing (it's the shoemaker's sign for search).
3. **`og:image` is a relative path** — link previews (texts, Slack, socials)
   likely show no image. Should be `https://beingsound.studio/images/…`.
4. **`id="about-text"` is reused dozens of times** — IDs must be unique;
   it works today but is fragile. A find-replace to `class="about-text"`
   someday.
5. **Missing alt text on the section images** (the covers have it).
6. **The 404.html copy step** — documented in MAINTENANCE.md at checkpoint 5;
   until then it's the one way to silently break QR links.
